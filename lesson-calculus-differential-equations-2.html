Separate, Integrate, Solve

Whenever you can herd all the y terms to one side and all the t terms to the other, solving the equation is just two integrals.

Spotting separability

An equation is separable when the right side factors into a pure function of x times a pure function of y. The equation y prime equals x times y is separable, while y prime equals x plus y is not, since that sum cannot be factored that way. Learning to see the factorization is most of the battle.

y' = g(x)\,h(y)

The three-step method

First rewrite the equation so every y sits with dy and every x sits with dx. Second, integrate both sides. For y prime equals x times y, dividing by y and integrating gives the log of the absolute value of y equals x squared over 2 plus C. Third, solve for y by exponentiating to get y equals A times e to the x squared over 2.

\int \frac{dy}{y} = \int x\,dx

Lost solutions and checks

Dividing by h of y can silently discard constant solutions, such as y equal to 0 in the example above, so check those separately. Use the initial condition to pin down the constant A. Finally, verify by differentiating your answer; a solution must reproduce the original equation exactly.

y = A\,e^{x^2/2}

Form of a separable differential equation

The slope function must split into a product of a function of x alone and a function of y alone. Once it does, the variables can be pulled onto opposite sides of the equation.
