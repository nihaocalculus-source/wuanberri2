Chain rule and implicit forms

When functions are stacked inside each other, differentiate the outside, leave the inside alone, then multiply by the inside's derivative.

The chain rule

If y is a function of u and u is a function of x, the derivative of the composition is the outside derivative evaluated at the inside, times the inside derivative. In words: differentiate outer, keep inner, multiply by derivative of inner. This handles composites like sin of 3x^2 in one step.

(f(g(x)))' = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)

Leibniz form

In Leibniz notation the chain rule reads dy/dx equals dy/du times du/dx, and the du terms cancel as if they were fractions. This form makes nested rates multiply, which is why it is the workhorse of related rates problems. Treat any intermediate variable as the u.

\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}

Implicit differentiation

For curves defined by an equation in x and y, differentiate both sides with respect to x, applying the chain rule to every y term so each contributes a dy/dx factor. Then solve algebraically for dy/dx. For the circle x^2 + y^2 = 25, differentiating gives 2x + 2y (dy/dx) = 0, so dy/dx = -x/y.

x^2 + y^2 = 25 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y}

Flashcard

(f \circ g)'(x) = f'(g(x)) g'(x)

Chain rule for composite functions

Differentiate the outer function with the inner left untouched, then multiply by the derivative of the inner function. The order of operations in the composition dictates the order of differentiation.
