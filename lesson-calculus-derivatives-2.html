Power, exponential, trig rules

A handful of short rules replace limit computations for the functions you meet most often.

Power rule

For any real number n, the derivative of x^n is n times x^(n-1). The exponent comes down as a multiplier and drops by one. Differentiating a polynomial reduces to applying this rule term by term, since constants multiply through and sums split apart.

\frac{d}{dx} x^n = n x^{n-1}

Exponential functions

The exponential with base e is its own derivative, which is what makes e special: changing the input by a factor multiplies the output by a constant. Other bases carry a factor of the natural logarithm of the base. The general exponential a^x differentiates to a^x times ln a.

\frac{d}{dx} e^x = e^x, \quad \frac{d}{dx} a^x = a^x \ln a

Trig derivatives

The derivative of sin x is cos x, and the derivative of cos x is negative sin x; the two cycle with a period of four differentiations. The derivative of tan x is secant squared of x. Combine these with the power rule term by term for mixed functions.

(\sin x)' = \cos x, \quad (\cos x)' = -\sin x

\frac{d}{dx} x^n = n x^{n-1}

Power rule for any real exponent

Bring the exponent down as a coefficient and reduce it by one. It works for negative and fractional exponents too, so roots and reciprocals need rewriting before differentiating.
